You were born

September 28, 1999. Exactly three years and four days after me.

The day you were born is probably my earliest memory. I don’t remember the hospital or the car ride, or much else about that day. But I remember sitting in a chair, my little feet dangling above the floor, while Dad carefully placed a small bundle in my arms. He kept holding you too, to make sure you were safe.

I don’t remember much, but I vividly remember holding you.

Ever since we were kids, I’ve thought of you as my favourite birthday present I ever got.

You completed our family

From day one, you belonged. Your arrival brought a balance to our family that I don’t quite know how to explain, except that it felt right with you there.

We wouldn’t have been the same Wilkinsons from the blue bungalow in rural Ontario without you. You helped make us who we were.

The four of us.

You have always brought joy with you.

You were such a happy baby. Not in the sense that you never cried or were somehow perfectly easy, but you were so often smiling. You had this naturally cheerful, positive presence that people could feel when you were in the room.

Looking back at photos of you from that time, I can still see it so clearly. That brightness was already there.

You’ve always been my birthday buddy

Growing up, having birthdays four days apart meant arguing over what kind of cake we’d share, who would come to the party, and who got to blow out the candles first. Very serious birthday business.

But for a long time now, sharing that little corner of September with you has felt like a blessing. It’s like stepping into the unknown of a new year of life with someone right there beside me.

If I could choose anyone in the world to be my birthday buddy, I’d choose you.

You have always loved with your whole heart.

You have always worn your heart on your sleeve. Even when you were three, you were a little romantic, sweet and completely sincere about the people you loved. At the time, you actually had two boyfriends. One of them was Noah, the little boy in this photo. I still remember Noah’s big sister and me deciding that the two of you obviously needed to get married, so we held a very official, completely made-up wedding ceremony for you in their backyard pool.

You were such a funny, affectionate little kid, and that softness has always been part of you. You have never been someone who loves halfway.

You have always had a style that is completely your own.

You have always had such a strong sense of style. Even as a baby, apparently. You’ve always had your own look, your own aesthetic, and a way of making things feel distinctly you.

That has stayed true through every version of you, including the teenage years and some admittedly questionable contouring choices. 😂 But it has never felt like vanity. You just have this natural beauty about you, and a way of moving through the world with style and grace.

Tu as appris le français avec moi.

We were two kids from an English-speaking household put into fully French school. Dad could introduce himself in French with some difficulty, and Mom knew a little more, but whenever I needed help with my homework, I asked you.

We would read together, and you helped me a lot with my pronunciation. You were patient with me, corrected me when I got things wrong, and somehow never seemed to get annoyed when I struggled.

Even though you were younger than me and learning too, you were still the person I went to when I needed help.

You made the middle of nowhere feel less lonely.

We grew up pretty much in the middle of nowhere, which meant that when we were home, it was usually just the two of us. Before school, after school, on weekends, and through those endless summer days, we spent so much time together.

We read, played, watched things, made up games, annoyed each other, and generally found ways to fill the hours. I don’t think I really appreciated at the time how much company we were to each other. Home could have felt incredibly isolated, but I had you there.

You have always made me laugh.

You have always had this way of making things funnier just by being yourself. The faces, the reactions, the little comments, the way you commit completely to whatever bit you’re doing. You can be ridiculous in the best possible way.

Some of my favourite memories with you are funny for reasons that would probably make no sense to anyone else. Half the time, I don’t even remember the exact joke anymore. I just remember laughing with you until whatever we were doing became ten times funnier than it had any right to be.

You have always made me laugh, and I hope you never stop.

You kept Christmas magical.

I figured out pretty young that Santa wasn’t real, and after that, part of Christmas became helping preserve the magic for you. You believed in it longer than I did, and honestly, I think that made Christmas more fun for me too.

You would wake us up at some completely unreasonable hour, usually around five in the morning, ready to open presents. I can still picture you kneeling beside the tree, practically vibrating with excitement next to the pile with your name on it, while we waited for Dad to get his coffee and Mom to get the camera.

One year we got a Wii and spent basically the entire day playing the boxing minigame. The next morning, on Boxing Day of all days, neither of us could move our arms because we were so sore.

You made Christmas feel like an event. Your excitement was contagious, and some of my favourite memories of the holidays are really memories of watching you enjoy them.

You grew up beside me.

In so many ways, we have always been opposites. We are different in temperament, different in how we carry ourselves, different in how we move through the world. In some ways, we are almost night and day.

And yet, somehow, there has always been so much overlap too. We grew up side by side, sharing the same road, the same home, the same routines, the same milestones, and so much of the same history. We were never the same person, but we were always shaped by so many of the same places and moments.

I love this photo because it feels like such a perfect picture of that. The two of us are standing side by side, headed in the same direction, carrying different backpacks, already our own distinct little people. That has always been us.

You have never been my mirror. You have always been your own person. But I got to grow up beside you, and that has meant everything.

You were number 7.

Grandma had seven grandchildren, and sometimes she got our names mixed up. When she was trying to get to the right one, she would work her way through this whole family Rolodex:

Deborah,Donna,Siobhan,Kelly,Tammy,Hannah,Fiona.

Eventually, it became easier to just think of us by number. I was number 6. You were number 7.

You were the youngest child of Grandma’s youngest child, and the youngest grandchild in the family. There was something fitting about you being the one at the end of the list, the last little piece added to that generation.

And somehow, even after Grandma had gone through everybody else first, eventually she always got to Fiona. 😂

You drove me absolutely nuts.

For a while there, you took your responsibilities as the annoying younger sister very seriously. As teenagers, we could fight like wildcats. I’m sure you could give your own account of how delightful I was to live with.

But eventually, we always made things better. Sometimes it took time. We weren’t always ready to understand each other or let something go right away, but being angry with you never meant I stopped loving you.

You could drive me absolutely nuts and still be one of my favourite people. Apparently, both things can be true.

You made Mexico memorable.

From the cornrows on the beach, to the near-constant self-serve ice cream we both absolutely abused, to the peacock chasing you, to you running toward the ocean and immediately encountering a beach full of naked Europeans, some of my funniest memories from that trip are memories of being there with you.

This photo still makes me smile, not just because of the trip itself, but because of the story that came with it. You told me the monkey had peed on the woman who got her picture taken right before you, which somehow makes your calm little smile here even funnier.

You made the whole trip feel bigger, sillier and more memorable just by being there.

You carry the best of them, and something all your own.

I see so much of the best of Mom and Dad in you. Dad’s intelligence and wit. Mom’s charisma and kindness. Familiar pieces of the people we grew up with, coming together in you.

And then there’s your spirit. Something in the way you laugh, care about things, and throw yourself into life that is entirely your own. I don’t quite know how to put it into words, but I know it when I’m around you.

I recognize parts of them in you, but that particular spark is uniquely Fiona. And I love getting to see the world with you in it.

It was never really about makeup or hair, though. It was another one of those ways you looked after me. You had the patience and the skill to help me feel a little more confident walking out the door, without ever making me feel like I needed to be someone other than myself.

You are in all my best memories.

When I look back at the happiest parts of my childhood, you are almost always there somewhere.

In old photos, in holidays, in ordinary afternoons, in ridiculous little moments that somehow became permanent memories, you are such a constant presence. Some of my favourite memories are not even really about anything important. They are just memories of being happy, and you happen to be there beside me.

I think that is one of the reasons so many of those memories still feel warm to me. You are woven through so much of the best of my past.

You found your way back to me.

There were years when we weren’t close. We missed pieces of each other’s lives, and there is no way to go back and recover that time.

But somehow, we found each other again.

Getting to know you again as adults has been one of the greatest gifts of my life. You weren’t just the little sister I remembered, and I wasn’t the same person you had known either. We both had years of growing and changing behind us. In a way, we had to meet each other again.

And we did.

Slowly, through conversations, stupid pictures, little updates, jokes, and all the ordinary things that rebuild familiarity, you became part of my everyday life again. Not because we were trying to recreate exactly what we had as kids, but because we were building something new between the people we had become.

I will always wish we hadn’t lost those years. But I am so grateful that losing time didn’t mean losing you forever.

You became the kind of teacher people remember.

You don’t just teach because it’s your job. You genuinely care about your students.

Having a smaller class means you get the chance to know them more deeply, and you actually take that opportunity. You learn who they are, what they struggle with, what they need, and what might help something finally click for them. You want them to succeed because you care about them, not just about whether they pass.

I know how much work you put into creating things for your students, finding different ways to teach something when one approach isn’t working, and trying to make your classroom a place where they feel supported and capable.

There are teachers people forget almost as soon as they leave their classroom, and then there are teachers whose names they still remember years later because that person made them feel seen.

I think you’re going to be one of those teachers.

After all the difficult shit life handed you, you still kept going. You kept learning, kept working, kept building a future for yourself, and became such an amazing and accomplished person.

You made losing Dad survivable.

There is nothing good I can say about losing Dad. I wish with all my heart that you had never had to experience a loss like that, especially when you were only nine years old.

But after he died, we became incredibly close. We were both hurting, both trying to understand how our lives could possibly keep going without him, and somehow we started taking care of each other.

Neither of us could fix what had happened. We were just kids, and we were grieving in our own ways. But we had each other. We kept each other company, looked out for each other, and became a kind of safe place for one another during the worst time of our lives.

Losing Dad was unbearable. Having you beside me made it survivable.

You fell in love

I remember when you were still waiting for your person. You’d pull tarot cards, looking for a little glimpse of when love might find you. I wished it for you with everything I had. My kind, beautiful, intelligent sister deserved someone who would see everything she was and treasure her.

Then came Ben.

Now you have someone who treats you like a queen, and I have an amazing brother-in-law. You deserve the happiness you’ve found. Seeing you loved like this makes me happier than I can write about without crying a little.

You're my sister.

You’re my person. I have looked up to you for as long as I can remember, even though you’re younger than me.

Growing up, before I understood why the world so often felt confusing to me, I watched you. You seemed to know how to move through it in ways that I didn’t yet understand. You gave me an example to follow when I desperately needed one. I learned from you, sometimes without either of us realizing that was what was happening.

And I still look up to you now. I look at the way you treat people, the way you teach, the way you love, and the care you put into the person you are. You have this combination of kindness, strength, humour and grace that I have admired in every version of you. Knowing you has made me a better person, not because you ever set out to teach me how to be one, but because loving you gave me someone worth aspiring to be more like.

There are billions of people in this world who could know you, love you, learn from you, laugh with you and be changed by knowing you. But somehow, out of everyone in the universe, I’m the one who gets to call you my sister.

I don’t think I will ever stop being grateful for that.
